How much monthly cash flow can a $350K budget realistically produce from an IOP?

Revised scope: the original goal was $50K/month cash flow. This brief answers the new question — what a fixed $350,000 budget can actually fund and earn from a single Intensive Outpatient Program (IOP / "day treatment") in behavioral health.

The short version

A lean IOP costs $60K–$150K to start. Your $350K is roughly 2–3× that — so money is not the constraint on launching one site. The constraint is census and payer mix. One well-run site tops out around $15K–$30K/month in net cash flow. To reach $50K/month you need a second site, a higher-acuity PHP (day-treatment) track at higher per-diems, or both — which is a $600K–$900K total plan, not $350K.

A single-site IOP plan splits the budget into one-time launch costs and a working-capital reserve that carries you until reimbursement turns on (in-network contracting and CARF accreditation realistically take 6–12 months).

The revenue engine

IOP bills a per-diem (CPT/HCPCS H0015, ≥180 minutes of programming) typically 3 days/week. Patients stay 8–16 weeks. Revenue scales almost entirely with average daily census — the number of patients actively enrolled and attending.

Most realistic sequencing on $350K: launch one in-network IOP, get it to 25–30 census and ~$20–27K/month, then reinvest that cash flow to fund site #2 or a PHP track toward the $50K goal — rather than trying to buy $50K of capacity up front with $350K.

Key assumptions & risks

  • Payer mix is the swing factor. Cash-pay nets ~$189/day; commercial $225–$285; Medicare/RHC ~$259. A weak commercial mix pushes break-even census higher.
  • Reimbursement lag. CARF accreditation and in-network contracting take 6–12 months — that's why a working-capital reserve, not just startup cost, defines the real budget.
  • Census ramp is the hard part. Filling 20+ census depends on referral relationships (hospitals, EAPs, courts, schools), not just opening doors. Budget real marketing time.
  • Staffing leverage is thin. Margins of 16–19% mean one clinician leaving can erase a quarter's profit. Plan redundancy.
  • Geography matters. Metro markets raise both per-diems and rent/payroll — net margin doesn't necessarily improve.

Bottom line

With $350K you can comfortably open and sustain one in-network IOP and, at maturity (12–18 months), pull $15K–$27K/month in net cash flow at 20–30 census. That single site will not produce $50K/month — that target needs ~2× the capacity (a second site or a PHP track), i.e. a $500K–$900K plan. The capital-efficient route is to fund one site now and let its cash flow finance expansion toward $50K.
