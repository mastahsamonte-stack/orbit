Starting an IDD Residential Business in Petersburg, VA

Licensing · Private pay vs. Medicaid · Rates · Home capacity & bedrooms · Feasibility

Research brief · June 2026

Short answer: Yes — feasible, and Petersburg is a reasonable target. Demand is strong (a 14,000+ person statewide waitlist), zoning is favorable for homes of 8 or fewer residents, and real estate is cheap. The real hurdles are operational, not market-based: getting your DBHDS license (6–12 months), staffing direct-care workers, and thin Medicaid margins.

1 Do you need a license / permit / certification?

Yes — a DBHDS provider license is mandatory before you can operate, period. This is set by statute, not optional.

  • The agency is the Virginia Department of Behavioral Health & Developmental Services (DBHDS), Office of Licensing. It licenses providers of developmental disability services (includes intellectual disability).
  • Legal basis: Va. Code § 37.2-405 — "No provider shall establish, conduct, maintain, or operate… any service, without being licensed…"
  • Governing regulation: 12VAC35-105, last amended Dec 1, 2025.

What the application involves

  • Submitted through the DBHDS CONNECT provider portal
  • Criminal background + Central Registry checks for all direct-care staff
  • Human Rights policies + complaint-resolution policy
  • On-site inspection of physical plant, records, service descriptions
  • Proof of HCBS settings compliance
  • Timeline: 6–12 months (up to ~9 months on a waitlist before a reviewer is even assigned)
⚠ Fees flag: Confirmed a $500 fee for children's residential providers; couldn't independently verify the exact current adult DD group-home fee. Confirm the current fee schedule with the DBHDS Office of Licensing.

2 Private pay vs. Medicaid — does that change it?

This is the thing people get wrong: the DBHDS license is triggered by providing the service — NOT by who pays.

ScenarioWhat you need
Private pay only (no Medicaid)DBHDS license still required. You can't legally operate for unrelated clients without it.
Billing Medicaid (DD Waiver)DBHDS license + DMAS Medicaid provider enrollment + provider agreement + HCBS compliance

Two separate layers:

  • DBHDS license = authorization to legally operate.
  • DMAS Medicaid enrollment = authorization to bill Medicaid & get paid. It doesn't let you operate; it assumes you already hold the license.

Order is one-directional: DBHDS license first → then DMAS enrollment. The three DD waivers are Building Independence, Family & Individual Supports, and Community Living (Community Living funds 24/7 group-home residential).

Exemptions: A narrow carve-out exists for a relative caring for a related individual — not for running a paid group home for unrelated clients. No "few clients = exempt" loophole. A "host home" / sponsored-residential setup isn't an exemption either — it runs under an agency's DBHDS license.

⚠ Flag: The exact § 37.2-405(B) exemption wording couldn't be pulled (state sites block automated access). Verify directly if a family/relative arrangement is in play.

3 The rates

How it's structured

  • Tiered (up to 4 tiers), assigned from 7 support levels based on the Supports Intensity Scale (SIS). Higher needs = higher tier = higher pay.
  • Geographic: Northern Virginia (NOVA) vs. Rest of State (ROS). Petersburg = Rest of State (the lower band).
  • Group home rates also vary by licensed bed size; billed ~344 days/year.

For reference, older confirmed Sponsored Residential tiers (2016 baseline, now outdated/low): Tier 1 $131.98 → Tier 4 $301.19/day.

⚠ Important: The full current grid (all tiers × group home / sponsored / supported living × NOVA/ROS) lives only in the official DMAS rate file, which blocks automated access. Get exact numbers from the DMAS Rates & Rate Setting page or the DD Waiver helpline 800-552-8627.
  • CCC Plus waiver does NOT fund group homes — congregate residential is exclusively a DD Waiver benefit.
  • Private-pay IDD rates aren't publicly published in VA. For context, senior assisted living medians ~$6,500/mo. You'd set your own price — no published benchmark.

Note: the zoning statute uses an 8-resident threshold, but the DD Waiver reimbursement cap for a new group home is 6 beds. New group homes practically top out at 6.

Bedrooms — can they be shared?

✓ Yes, shared bedrooms are allowed. A private bedroom is NOT required by VA licensing (12VAC35-105-340):

  • Max 2 individuals per bedroom in a group home (up to 4 in other residential types).
  • Single bedroom: ≥ 80 sq ft. Shared: ≥ 60 sq ft per person.
  • Each resident needs adequate private storage.
  • Bathrooms: at least 1 toilet, 1 sink, 1 shower/bath per 4 residents.
Federal overlay (HCBS Settings Rule, 42 CFR 441.301): Shared rooms are allowed, but for Medicaid-funded homes each resident must be offered a private-unit option, get to choose their roommate if sharing, have a lockable bedroom door, and control their schedule/visitors/food access. You can do shared bedrooms — you just can't force it.
⚠ Flag: The "7 total occupants" figure for sponsored residential couldn't be pulled verbatim; "2 individuals served" is solidly confirmed. Verify the occupant count directly if it matters.

5 Is Petersburg, VA feasible?

Yes, with caveats. The market signals are genuinely favorable.

Demand — strong

  • Statewide DD Waiver waitlist ~14,258 (Nov 2025), ~2,727 Priority 1.
  • 2024 General Assembly funded 3,440 new Priority 1 slots (FY25–26), releasing through Dec 2026 — each is a potential resident.
  • Petersburg is high-poverty (~21.7%), high-Medicaid — above-average eligible population.

Your entry point — the local CSB

  • Served by District 19 CSB ("Greater Reach") — 20 W. Bank Street, Petersburg; (804) 862-6082.
  • Critical: the CSB (not you) controls who gets a waiver slot and assigns the Support Coordinators who refer residents. You fill beds by building relationships with D19CSB support coordinators — not by just opening beds.

Zoning — a real advantage

  • Va. Code § 15.2-2291: a licensed home of 8 or fewer residents is treated as "single-family residential" — by-right in single-family zones, no special-use permit or rezoning.
⚠ Flag: Couldn't confirm whether a spacing/separation requirement between homes applies (not in §15.2-2291 itself). Check Title 37.2 licensing regs before siting two homes close together.

Competition

Not empty, not saturated — ~5 existing operators in the Tri-Cities (A Village Youth & Family Services, Faithful Residential, Family Services, Independent Capacity System, Virginia Group Homes Services). Given the waitlist, there's room for a differentiated entrant (e.g., medically/behaviorally complex populations).

The real risks

  • Direct Support Professional (DSP) shortage — #1 statewide operational risk; high turnover.
  • Licensing barrier — 6–12 months before you can bill.
  • Thin Medicaid margins / rate dependency.
  • Census depends on CSB referrals, not on you.

Bottom line

  • License? Yes — DBHDS license required either way (private pay or Medicaid). Medicaid adds DMAS enrollment on top.
  • Rates? Tiered by support level + NOVA/ROS. Petersburg = Rest of State; top-tier group home ~$277/day (2024), ~$285/day now. Get the exact grid from DMAS.
  • Residents per home? 6 beds for a new DD Waiver group home (8 is the zoning threshold).
  • Bedrooms? Shared allowed — max 2/room in a group home, 60 sq ft/person — but residents must be offered a private option & choose their roommate.
  • Petersburg? Feasible and well-chosen: strong demand, by-right zoning, cheap real estate. Win or lose on DSP staffing & D19CSB referrals.

Recommended first three moves

  1. Call the DBHDS Office of Licensing to confirm the current DD group-home fee and start the CONNECT application.
  2. Pull the official DMAS SFY26 rate file or call 800-552-8627 for exact tier rates.
  3. Introduce yourself to District 19 CSB support coordinators to understand local referral flow.
