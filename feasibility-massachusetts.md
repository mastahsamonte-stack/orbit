# Feasibility Study — Assisted Living Facility for the Neurodivergent — Massachusetts (2026-06-02)

> **Scope:** State-level screen for Massachusetts (statewide). A state-level
> study cannot clear a specific site: **municipal zoning, local permitting,
> building/fire-code occupancy classification, and neighborhood crime/competition
> must still be verified per address** before committing. Treat this as a
> go/no-go on *the state*, plus a roadmap of what to confirm locally.

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Verdict

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL GO** — Massachusetts has genuine, documented demand and a
favorable zoning/safety profile, but the path you choose to *get paid* determines
whether this is viable. The decisive factors:

1. **Demand is real and structural.** MA has the **highest adult autism
   prevalence in the U.S. (~2.42%)**, DDS serves ~32,000 adults, ~8,000 people
   already live in group homes, and roughly **3,000 people are waiting** for
   day-program placements — with residential placements rationed to crisis cases
   first. Unmet need is not the problem.
2. **The funding model is the gating risk.** Public (MassHealth/DDS) residential
   dollars do **not** flow through an open fee-for-service market. They flow
   through **procurement** (Chapter 257, rates set under 101 CMR 424.00). A new
   operator generally must become a **qualified DDS provider** and **win contracts/
   placements** — you can't simply open beds and bill. This is the single biggest
   barrier to entry.
3. **Licensure pathway is a fork in the road.** "Assisted Living Residence" (ALR,
   elderly model, 651 CMR 12.00) and "DDS residential / group home" (IDD/autism
   model, 115 CMR 7.00) are **different agencies, rules, and money**. A
   neurodivergent-adult facility almost always belongs on the **DDS track**, not
   the elderly-ALR track. Getting this wrong invalidates the rest of the plan.
4. **Workforce is the operating risk.** Direct Support Professional (DSP)
   shortage is at crisis levels (~43% national turnover; 72% of MA providers
   report worsening hiring). Staffing — not rent — is your dominant cost and your
   biggest execution risk.
5. **Zoning and safety are tailwinds.** The **Dover Amendment** strongly protects
   group homes for the disabled from hostile local zoning, and MA is the **5th-
   safest state**. Both reduce two risks that kill these projects elsewhere.

**Bottom line:** Viable if you either (a) commit to the multi-year work of
becoming a contracted DDS provider, or (b) target a **private-pay neurodivergent
residential niche** (smaller payer pool, but no procurement gate). A blended model
is plausible. Do **not** proceed on an assumption of easy Medicaid billing.

## 2. Location Overview & Demographics

- **Statewide population:** ~7.0M.
- **Target population:** MA has the **highest measured adult autism prevalence in
  the nation (~2.42%)**, and among the highest male rates (~4.0%). Childhood ASD
  identification (~1 in 44 at age 8) historically ran above the national average,
  meaning a **large, growing cohort aging into adult services**.
- **DDS-served population:** ~**32,000 adults** and ~9,000 children with
  intellectual/developmental disabilities; ~**8,000 individuals** live in
  staffed group homes statewide supported by ~15,000 DSPs.

## 3. Regulatory & Licensing

**This is the most important section — resolve your pathway first.**

### Pathway A — DDS Residential / Group Home (the usual fit for neurodivergent adults)
- **Governing agency:** Department of Developmental Services (DDS), under EOHHS.
- **Core regulation:** **115 CMR 7.00** ("Standards for All Services and Supports")
  governs licensure and certification of provider-operated residences. Group homes
  are licensed residences typically for **3–6 adults**, staffed **24/7**.
- **Environmental/physical standards (115 CMR 7.07):** complete living
  accommodations (own kitchen, living/dining, bedrooms, baths of typical
  residential design), privacy, fire/chemical-hazard safety, vermin-free,
  appropriately sized.
- **Eligibility of residents:** DDS eligibility now expressly includes **adults
  with autism** (post-2014 autism eligibility expansion), in addition to
  intellectual disability.
- **Admission/level of care:** Residential Waiver placements prioritize 24/7
  behavioral/medical/physical-support needs; crisis cases (homelessness, lost
  caregiver) get first priority.

Sources: [115 CMR 7.00](https://www.mass.gov/doc/115-cmr-7-standards-for-all-services-and-supports/download), [115 CMR 7.07 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/115-CMR-7-07), [DDS eligibility for adults with autism](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/dds-eligibility-for-adults-with-autism)

### Pathway B — Assisted Living Residence (ALR) — *generally NOT the neurodivergent fit*
- **Governing agency:** Executive Office of Aging & Independence (formerly Elder
  Affairs / EOEA).
- **Core authority:** **M.G.L. c. 19D** and **651 CMR 12.00**. Certification, not
  medical licensure; **application at least 60 days before opening**, compliance
  review by the Secretary, **2-year renewal** terms.
- **Why it usually doesn't fit:** the ALR model is a *housing + limited personal
  care* model oriented to elders; it is **not** designed for the behavioral/
  developmental support profile of most neurodivergent adults. Use this track only
  if serving higher-independence adults on a private-pay basis.

Sources: [651 CMR 12.00 (full text)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/651-cmr-12-certification-procedures-and-standards-for-assisted-living-residences/download), [Assisted Living Certification Regulations](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/assisted-living-certification-regulations)

### Provider entry (Pathway A) — the real gate
- To receive DDS funding you must complete the **provider administrative & financial
  qualification** process **before** an RFR response can even be evaluated on
  COMMBUYS; qualification is valid for **12 months**. Then you compete for
  contracts/placements. See the DDS **"Overview for New Providers" (rev. Nov 2024)**
  and the **Purchase of Service** process.
- **Background checks, abuse-registry screening, incident reporting:** required under
  the DDS regulatory framework (confirm current CORI/DPPC requirements during
  qualification). *(Exact current checklist: verify with DDS.)*
- **Timeline & cost to licensure/qualification: DATA GAP** — obtain from DDS provider
  onboarding; budget many months and dedicated compliance staff.

Sources: [Overview for New Providers (DDS)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/overview-for-new-providers/download), [DDS Contracts (Purchase of Services)](https://www.mass.gov/dds-contracts-purchase-of-services)

## 4. Permitting & Zoning

- **Dover Amendment (M.G.L. c. 40A, § 3):** Massachusetts strongly protects
  educational and certain residential-care uses from discriminatory local zoning.
  Congregate living facilities for people with disabilities have qualified as
  protected uses. Where Dover applies, a town may enforce **only reasonable
  dimensional rules** (bulk, height, setbacks, lot area, open space, parking,
  coverage) — **not** density, screening, traffic, or signage restrictions aimed at
  the use. This is a major advantage versus states without such protection.
- **Anti-discrimination:** Local land-use/health-safety rules "shall not
  discriminate against a disabled person."
- **Federal Fair Housing Act:** independently requires **reasonable accommodation**
  in zoning for housing for people with disabilities — a second layer of protection.
- **Building/fire code (780 CMR):** the facility's **use & occupancy classification**
  (e.g., residential **R** vs. institutional **I-1**) depends on residents' capacity
  for self-preservation and the level of care — this drives sprinkler/egress/
  construction requirements and **must be determined per building** with the local
  building official. NFPA 101 life-safety applies.
- **Per-site permitting (DATA GAP until an address is chosen):** special permit/
  variance needs, occupancy limits, parking, septic/Title 5 if not on sewer.

Sources: [M.G.L. c.40A §3](https://malegislature.gov/laws/generallaws/parti/titlevii/chapter40a/section3), [Dover Amendment overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover_Amendment), [780 CMR Ch.3 Use & Occupancy](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/780-CMR-CHAPTER-3)

## 5. Reimbursement & Rates

### Public (MassHealth / DDS)
- **HCBS waivers for adults with IDD:** three programs — **Adult Supports**,
  **Community Living**, and **Intensive Supports** waivers — administered by EOHHS/
  MassHealth with DDS as lead. The **Residential Waiver/Intensive Supports** track
  funds 24/7 staffed residential placements.
- **Financial eligibility (2025):** countable assets **≤ $2,000**; countable income
  **≤ 300% of SSI FBR (~$2,901/mo in 2025)**. (This is the *resident's* eligibility,
  not the provider's.)
- **Rate-setting:** residential and support-service rates are **model-based, set by
  EOHHS under Chapter 257 of the Acts of 2008** and codified at **101 CMR 424.00**
  ("Rates for Certain Developmental and Support Services"). Rates are *administered*,
  not negotiated freely, and published in DDS Chapter 257 rate tables.
- **Specific per-diem residential rate $$: DATA GAP** — pull the current figure from
  **101 CMR 424.00** and the DDS Chapter 257 rate tables for the exact service code
  (Adult Long-Term Residential), since rates vary by service class/acuity and update
  periodically.

### Private pay
- **MA median assisted living: ~$7,250/month** (range ~$6,600–$8,000), among the
  highest in the U.S. Regional spread: ~$6,368/mo (Springfield) to ~$9,825/mo
  (Pittsfield); Greater Boston typically above the midpoint. Annual increases run
  ~3–7%.
- A **private-pay neurodivergent residential** offering would price against this
  market but command a premium for specialized 24/7 staffing — *if* families can pay.

## 6. Level of Care / Need

- **Acuity served:** 24/7 staffed residential for adults whose behavioral, medical,
  and/or physical support needs prevent more independent living — matched to the DDS
  Residential/Intensive Supports tier.
- **Documented unmet need:** MA officially keeps **no formal waitlist**, but that
  understates demand — placements are rationed, **~3,000 people wait for day
  programs**, and residential slots prioritize crisis cases. Referral pipelines:
  DDS area offices, hospital discharge planners, school-to-adult transition (age-22
  cliff), guardianship organizations, and family advocacy groups (The Arc, AANE).
- **Implication:** Demand validation is strong; the constraint is supply of
  *staffed, funded* placements — exactly the gap a competent operator fills.

Sources: [Workforce/waitlist context](https://thearcofmass.org/workforce/), [DDS adult autism support](https://www.mass.gov/dds-adult-autism-support)

## 7. Crime & Safety

- **Massachusetts ranks #5 safest state** (2024). Statewide **violent crime
  308.8/100k**, **property crime 1,101.2/100k**; Part-One crime fell ~4.4% in 2024.
- **Avoid / scrutinize:** Holyoke and Springfield (Hampden County) show the highest
  city crime rates (well above state average). Western-MA Gateway Cities and some
  urban cores carry elevated risk.
- **Favorable:** MetroWest, suburban Middlesex/Norfolk/Worcester-county towns, and
  much of the North Shore combine low crime with service infrastructure.
- **Implication for a vulnerable-resident facility:** site selection should weight
  neighborhood safety heavily; the statewide profile is a tailwind, but
  **neighborhood-level crime must be checked per address (DATA GAP until a site is
  named).**

Sources: [MA crime statistics](https://www.mass.gov/crime-statistics), [2024 downward trend](https://www.mass.gov/news/massachusetts-crime-rates-continue-downward-trend-ahead-of-summer-season-preliminary-2024-data-shows-fewer-violent-and-property-crimes-statewide), [Safest/most-dangerous cities](https://crimegrade.org/safest-places-in-massachusetts/)

## 8. Market Analysis

- **Demand/supply:** Senior-housing analog signals a **supply-constrained, high-
  occupancy market**: **Boston had the highest occupancy of major U.S. markets at
  93.1%**, national assisted-living occupancy 87.7% (Q4 2025), with **inventory
  growth under 1%** and few new units opening. MA is explicitly a **"high barriers
  to entry"** market.
- **Read-across to neurodivergent residential:** while DDS residential is a distinct
  (largely public-funded) market, the same drivers — constrained new supply, high
  construction/labor cost, strong demand — apply. Barriers to entry that constrain
  competitors also protect an operator who clears them.
- **Growth outlook:** demographic tailwind (children aging into adult services +
  aging family caregivers) plus chronic placement shortage = durable demand.

Sources: [NIC 2025 occupancy](https://www.nic.org/news-press/occupancy-rate-for-senior-living-communities-increased-in-2025-as-construction-stalled/), [NIC MAP 2Q25 — record-low inventory growth](https://www.nicmap.com/blog/senior-housing-occupancy-rises-in-2q-2025-inventory-growth-at-record-lows/)

Established, well-capitalized **nonprofit** incumbents dominate DDS-funded
residential. Major players:

| Provider | Approx. residential footprint | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Seven Hills Foundation** | 130+ group home settings (MA/RI) | Largest; IDD + acquired brain injury |
| **Riverside Community Care** | 33 group homes (eastern MA) | IDD/autism/ABI, 24/7 |
| **The Guild for Human Services** | 15 residential homes (eastern MA) | IDD/DD adults |
| **Charles River Center** | Autism-specialized group homes | Young adults w/ ASD |
| **Bridgewell** | Multiple homes incl. autism + medically-intensive | North Shore |
| **Devereux**, **Northeast Arc**, **Amego**, **JRI**, **Tempus Unlimited** | Various | Among revenue leaders |

- **Competitive reality:** these are entrenched, mission-driven, contract-holding
  nonprofits. As a **new for-profit/startup entrant**, you compete for the *same DDS
  contracts and placements*, which favors incumbents with track records.
- **Differentiation opportunities / gaps:** (a) **autism-specific, behaviorally
  specialized** homes for adults without intellectual disability (an underserved
  niche distinct from generic IDD group homes); (b) **medically-intensive +
  behavioral** hybrid models (Bridgewell signals demand); (c) **private-pay
  premium** residential for families who don't qualify for or won't wait on DDS
  placement; (d) geographic gaps where incumbents are thin.

## 10. Real Estate

- **Model:** typical group home = ordinary residential dwelling (3–6 residents) of
  "typical residential design" — favors acquiring/leasing single-family or large
  homes rather than institutional construction. Dover protection eases siting.
- **Cost:** MA real estate is expensive, especially Greater Boston; western/central
  MA offers lower acquisition cost but thinner labor markets.
- **Retrofit needs:** accessibility (ADA/aging-in-place), fire/life-safety per 780
  CMR occupancy class (sprinklers, egress), and any sensory/behavioral design for
  neurodivergent residents.

## 11. Financial Feasibility (high-level)

**Cost structure is staffing-dominated.** For a 24/7 home of 4–6 residents you must
staff around the clock (often 2:1 to 1:3 ratios depending on acuity), so **payroll
is 70%+ of operating cost**.

- **Startup (rough, verify):** provider qualification + compliance build-out, real
  estate acquisition/lease + retrofit, licensing, initial working capital to cover
  payroll before reimbursement stabilizes. **DATA GAP — model with real local
  comps.**
- **Operating cost drivers:** DSP wages (sector pushing toward **$24/hr**), benefits,
  turnover/overtime (turnover ~43% nationally), supervision/clinical, insurance,
  occupancy.
- **Revenue:** DDS per-diem (model-based, 101 CMR 424.00) × occupied beds, or
  private-pay (~$7,250/mo benchmark, premium for specialization). Margins on DDS
  contracts are thin by design.
- **Break-even sketch:** drive to **full occupancy** and **contain staffing cost/
  turnover**; on the DDS track, revenue is capped by administered rates, so margin
  comes from operational efficiency, not pricing power. Private-pay offers pricing
  power but a smaller payer pool.

> **Key assumption to validate:** that you can secure DDS placements (Pathway A) OR
> a sufficient private-pay census (Pathway C). Everything else is secondary.

Sources: [Workforce shortage / wage goal](https://thearcofmass.org/workforce/), [Genworth/CareScout costs](https://www.carescout.com/cost-of-care)

## 12. Risks & Open Questions

| Risk | Severity | Next verification step |
|---|---|---|
| **Procurement gate** — can't bill DDS without qualification + winning contracts | **High** | Contact DDS provider onboarding; read "Overview for New Providers" + POS manual |
| **Wrong licensure pathway** (ALR vs DDS) | **High** | Confirm with DDS which licensure (115 CMR 7.00) applies to your resident profile |
| **DSP workforce shortage / turnover** | **High** | Build a staffing/recruitment + wage model at $20–24/hr; stress-test turnover |
| **Thin administered margins** | Med-High | Pull exact 101 CMR 424.00 per-diem for the service code; model at realistic occupancy |
| **Entrenched nonprofit incumbents** | Medium | Map competitors + gaps in target county; define differentiation (autism-specific / private-pay) |
| **Per-site zoning/building code** | Medium | Once site chosen: confirm Dover applicability, 780 CMR occupancy class with building official |
| **Real estate cost (Greater Boston)** | Medium | Compare metro vs central/western MA cost-vs-labor trade-off |
| **Resident financial eligibility limits** | Low-Med | Confirm current SSI FBR, MA supplement, and waiver income/asset limits |

### Top open questions to answer before committing
1. **Public vs private pay** — are you pursuing DDS contracts, private-pay
   specialization, or a blend? This decision reframes the entire plan.
2. **What sub-population** — autism without ID, IDD, medically-intensive, or behavioral?
   Determines staffing, licensure tier, and competitive gap.
3. **What region/town** — drives crime, real estate, labor, and competitor density.
4. **Exact DDS per-diem rate** for the target service code (101 CMR 424.00).
5. **Provider-qualification timeline and cost** from DDS.
