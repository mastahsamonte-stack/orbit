## SECTION 0 — THE ONE THING TO GET RIGHT

> **Do NOT mass-blast LOIs to your whole list.** That is the single biggest mistake new
> RV park buyers make.

Here's why, and what to do instead:

| Mass-LOI blast (WRONG) | Staged outreach (RIGHT) |
|---|---|
| Sends a price before you've seen a single financial | Gathers financials first, prices second |
| Brokers blacklist you as "not a real buyer" | Brokers see you as serious and bring you off-market deals |
| Sellers feel insulted by a lowball you can't defend | Sellers open up because you asked smart questions first |
| You can't defend the number on a call | Every number you send is backed by a T12 and a model |
| 0.5% response, mostly angry | Higher response, real conversations |

**The correct sequence is a funnel, not a blast:**

```
List (10,000s)  →  Prioritize (hundreds)  →  Skip-trace/enrich  →
Soft first-touch  →  Conversation + request financials  →
UNDERWRITE  →  Verbal price range  →  LOI (only on deals that pencil)  →
PSA / Due Diligence  →  Close
```

LOIs are step 8, not step 1. Everything before the LOI exists to (a) figure out who's
actually motivated and (b) get the data you need to underwrite. **You earn the right to
send an LOI by doing the homework first.**

## SECTION 1 — BROKERS FIRST OR SELLERS FIRST? (Run BOTH, on separate tracks)

You asked whether to connect with brokers first or sellers first. The answer is **both, in
parallel, but they are two completely different motions.** Never use the same script on both.

### Track A — Direct-to-Seller (Off-Market)
The park owners on your national list, contacted directly. **This is where the best deals
are** (no broker, less competition, motivated mom-and-pop owners aging out).
- **Pro:** Less competition, room to negotiate creative terms (seller financing!), better basis.
- **Con:** Slow, owners often have messy/no books, you do all the work, harder to reach.
- **Goal of first contact:** NOT to buy. To start a relationship and learn if they'd ever sell.

### Track B — Broker / On-Market
Listed deals on Crexi, LoopNet, and through specialized RV park brokers. Build relationships
with the **10–15 brokers who dominate RV/MH park sales** so they call you *first* on pocket
listings.
- **Pro:** Deals come with a package (T12, rent roll) — fast to underwrite. Brokers pre-qualify.
- **Con:** More competition, priced closer to market, brokers test if you're a real buyer.
- **Goal of first contact:** Get on their buyer list and prove you can close, so you get
  early/off-market looks.

> **Why both:** Direct-to-seller fills the top of your funnel with cheap, creative-term deals.
> Brokers give you fast reps to learn underwriting and occasional gems. The broker
> relationships also make you *look* like a serious buyer to sellers.

## SECTION 2 — PRIORITIZE THE LIST BEFORE YOU MESSAGE ANYONE

Do not message the whole list. Score it and work the top tier first. Messaging is a limited
resource (your time, your sending reputation, compliance limits). Spend it on the parks most
likely to (a) sell and (b) pencil.

### Build a simple scoring model (1–5 each, sum = priority score)

| Factor | Why it matters | High score = |
|---|---|---|
| **# of sites** | Deals under ~50 sites are often too small to justify pro management; 50–150 is the sweet spot for a first acquisition | 50–150 sites |
| **Owner tenure** | Owners who've held 15+ years are aging out → motivated | Long-held |
| **Ownership type** | Individual / family LLC > REIT/institutional (won't sell to you) | Individual / small LLC |
| **Out-of-state owner** | Tired absentee owners sell | Owner address ≠ park address |
| **Utility setup** | Sub-metered or city utilities >> master-metered well/septic | City water/sewer, sub-metered |
| **Annual vs transient mix** | Some long-term tenants = stable base income | Mix of annual + transient |
| **Location / growth** | Path of growth, near attractions/employment, not flood zone | Growing metro / destination |
| **Online presence** | A dated GoogleSite + bad reviews = mismanaged = value-add | Looks mom-and-pop |

Tier the list:
- **Tier 1 (work now):** Score ≥ 30, 50–150 sites, individual owner, decent utilities.
- **Tier 2 (nurture):** Good park, wrong time / institutional / tiny.
- **Tier 3 (skip for now):** <20 sites, flood zone, ground-leased land, RV resort REIT-owned.

### Enrich Tier 1 before outreach (skip-tracing)
You need a **name + phone + email + mailing address** for the actual decision-maker.
- Pull the entity from the county/state **Secretary of State** business registry → find the
  registered agent / managing member.
- Pull the **parcel owner** from the **county assessor / GIS** → mailing address (often the
  owner's home, which confirms absentee).
- Skip-trace tools: BatchSkipTracing, TruePeopleSearch, REISkip, or a VA doing manual lookups.
- Verify the park is still operating (Google Maps, recent reviews, their booking site).

> **Rule:** A park is not "ready to contact" until it has a real person, a real number, and a
> reason it scored Tier 1. Garbage list → garbage campaign.

## SECTION 3 — THE FIRST TOUCH IS NOT AN OFFER (Direct-to-Seller cadence)

The first contact's only job is to **start a human conversation and find motivation.** You are
a buyer who loves their kind of park — not a wholesaler with a number.

### Channel priority (most → least compliant, see Section 8)
1. **Cold email** (lowest legal risk for B2B, scalable) — lead here.
2. **Direct mail / letter or yellow-letter** (high trust, low volume, great for absentee owners).
3. **Manual phone call** (best conversion, but call manually — no autodialer to cell phones).
4. **SMS** — only after opt-in or with extreme caution (highest TCPA risk; see Section 8).

### Recommended multi-touch sequence (per Tier-1 owner, over ~3 weeks)

| Day | Channel | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email #1 (intro) | Land softly, ask one easy question |
| 3 | Mail letter | Tangible, credible, hits absentee owners |
| 6 | Manual call #1 | "Did you get my note?" — start a conversation |
| 9 | Email #2 (value/why you) | Build credibility, social proof |
| 14 | Manual call #2 | Try a different time of day |
| 21 | Email #3 (break-up) | "Should I close your file?" — often gets a reply |

### SCRIPT — Cold Email #1 (Direct-to-Seller)
> **Subject:** Quick question about [Park Name]
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> I'll be brief — I'm a private buyer who focuses specifically on RV parks like
> [Park Name] in [City]. I'm not a broker and I'm not blasting a list; I came across your
> park and genuinely liked what you've built.
>
> I'm not asking you to sell. I'm just trying to learn the area. **If you ever did consider
> selling down the road, would you be open to a no-pressure conversation?** Even a "not in a
> million years" is a totally fine answer.
>
> Either way, thanks for what you do — these parks matter.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Phone] · [Email]
> *[Physical mailing address — required by CAN-SPAM]*
> *Not interested in hearing from me? Reply "stop" and I'll remove you. — required opt-out*

### SCRIPT — Manual Call #1 (Direct-to-Seller)
> "Hi [Name], this is [You] — I sent you a note about [Park Name]. I'm a buyer who focuses on
> RV parks and I'm not trying to pressure you at all. I'm just trying to understand the area
> and your park stood out. **Do you have 90 seconds?**
> ...
> Can I ask — how long have you owned it? ... What made you get into RV parks? ... Have you
> ever thought about what you'd do if the right offer came along someday?"

**What you're listening for (the only goal):** motivation (age, health, tired, partner
dispute, other ventures) and whether they keep books. If warm → move to Section 5
(info-gathering), NOT to a price.

## SECTION 4 — BROKER OUTREACH (Track B cadence)

Brokers are gatekeepers to on-market and pocket deals. Treat them as long-term relationships,
not transactions. Identify the **specialist RV/MH brokers** (the ones whose names keep
appearing on Crexi/LoopNet RV listings) plus the RV/MH teams at the big shops.

### SCRIPT — Broker Intro Email
> **Subject:** RV park buyer — [State/Region] — adding to your buyer list
>
> Hi [Broker],
>
> I'm an active buyer focused on RV parks in [region], targeting **[50–150]-site parks,
> [price range], [cap range]**. I can move on the right deal and I underwrite fast off a T12
> and rent roll.
>
> Could you add me to your buyer list? And if you have anything **off-market or pre-launch**
> that fits, I'd love first look. Happy to sign a confidentiality/registration agreement.
>
> [Name] · [Phone] · [Proof you're real: website, prior deals, or "JV-backed with funding"]

### Buy box to give every broker (one page, so they pattern-match for you)
- **Asset:** RV parks (open to MH/RV hybrids); will consider value-add and stabilized.
- **Size:** 50–150 sites (will look at 40–250).
- **Geography:** [your target states].
- **Price:** $[X]–$[Y].
- **Going-in cap:** [8%+] on actuals (state your minimum).
- **Utilities:** Prefer city or sub-metered; will consider well/septic with discount.
- **Structure:** All-cash close, but **strongly prefer seller financing** — flag those first.
- **Timeline:** [X] days to close, [X]-day inspection.

> **The fastest way to earn broker trust:** when they send a deal, send back a *real*
> underwriting question within 24 hours (about the T12, not "what's the lowest they'll take").
> That signals competence and gets you the next pocket listing.

## SECTION 5 — THE DEAL QUALIFIER (Get the data to underwrite)

Once a seller or broker is warm, you need a specific set of documents *before* you can price.
This is your **"Seller Information Packet" request.** Frame it as "so I can give you a real
number, not a lowball."

### Documents to request (the underwriting package)
1. **Trailing 12-month P&L (T12)** — income and expenses by month.
2. **Rent roll / site inventory** — every site: type (FHU 30/50 amp, partial, tent, cabin,
   storage), rate, transient vs annual, occupied vs vacant.
3. **Occupancy history** — last 2–3 years, ideally monthly (seasonality matters).
4. **2–3 years of tax returns or Schedule E** (to reconcile against the T12).
5. **Utility bills** — 12 months (especially electric/water if master-metered).
6. **List of amenities & ancillary income** — store, laundry, propane, cabins, boat/RV storage.
7. **Capex / deferred maintenance list** — roads, pads, hookups, septic, pool, buildings.
8. **Permits / licenses / zoning** — # of permitted sites, any non-conforming use.
9. **Property details** — acreage, water source (city vs well), sewer (city vs septic/lagoon),
   flood zone, expansion land.

> If a seller "doesn't have books," that is **not a dealbreaker — it's a value-add signal.**
> It means they're under-managed. Reconstruct income from the rent roll + occupancy + their
> bank deposits, and underwrite conservatively.

## SECTION 6 — UNDERWRITING RV PARKS (The model)

This is the core skill you're learning. Here's the framework to apply to every package you
receive. (Build this as a spreadsheet; a starter template is in Section 11.)

### Step 1 — Reconstruct REAL income
Don't trust the pro-forma. Build income from the rent roll × actual occupancy:
- **Site revenue:** transient (nightly/weekly) + annual/long-term (monthly) — model
  seasonality, not just peak.
- **Ancillary:** store, laundry, propane, cabins/rentals, storage, late fees, pet fees, Wi-Fi.
- **Use ECONOMIC occupancy**, not physical. A 90%-full park at half-market rates is a
  value-add, not a stabilized asset.

### Step 2 — Normalize expenses
RV parks typically run a **35%–50% expense ratio** (lower than apartments because transient
tenants turn fast and pay their own short-term utilities). Watch for:
- **Property taxes** — will likely *reset to your purchase price* on sale. Model the reassessed
  amount, not the seller's frozen basis. (Huge rookie error.)
- **Payroll / management** — even if owner-operated, **underwrite in a real management cost
  (8–12% or a manager salary + housing).** "Owner works for free" is not a real expense
  structure you can buy.
- **Utilities** — if master-metered, electric/water is on you. Sub-metering is a top value-add.
- **Insurance** — getting more expensive; get a real quote, don't trust trailing.
- **Repairs/maintenance + reserves** — budget reserves per site for pads, roads, hookups.
- Marketing, online booking fees (Campspot/etc.), credit card fees, admin, legal.

### Step 3 — NOI and value
```
NOI = Effective Gross Income − Operating Expenses   (exclude debt service & capex)

Value (income approach) = NOI ÷ Cap Rate
```
- **Cap rates:** RV parks commonly trade **7%–10%+**. Tertiary/value-add/mom-and-pop deals
  should be bought at a *higher* cap (cheaper); stabilized destination resorts trade tighter.
  **Underwrite to your minimum going-in cap on ACTUAL (in-place) NOI**, then upside is gravy.

### Step 4 — Sanity checks (always run all three)
1. **Income approach:** NOI ÷ cap (above) — the primary.
2. **Per-site value:** Purchase price ÷ # sites. Compare to recent comps ($/site).
3. **Price per occupied site / GRM** — quick gut check vs. the market.

### Step 5 — Returns & financing
- Model **debt** (agency/SBA/local bank/seller carry) → DSCR (lenders want ~1.25x+).
- Compute **cash-on-cash** and **5-yr IRR** with a realistic exit cap (exit cap ≥ entry cap).
- **Seller financing** changes everything — if the seller carries at a below-market rate, you
  can pay more and still hit returns. Always model an all-cash *and* a seller-carry scenario.

### The value-add levers (where RV park money is made)
| Lever | Effect |
|---|---|
| Raise rates to market | Direct NOI increase — biggest lever in mom-and-pop parks |
| Increase occupancy (online booking, marketing) | More EGI on the same cost base |
| Sub-meter utilities → bill back | Cuts your biggest variable expense |
| Add sites / use expansion land | New revenue on owned dirt |
| Add ancillary (storage, cabins, propane, store) | High-margin income |
| Professionalize management & reporting | Raises NOI *and* compresses exit cap |

### Red flags that kill deals
- Master-metered utilities with no sub-metering path.
- On **leased land** (you don't own the dirt).
- Flood zone / environmental (old gas station, lagoon sewer failing).
- Declining occupancy trend or one-customer concentration (e.g., one oilfield crew = 60%).
- Permitted-site count < actual sites in use (illegal/non-conforming).
- Seller numbers that can't be reconciled to tax returns or bank deposits.

## SECTION 7 — THE LOI (Last, not first)

You only send an LOI on a deal you've underwritten and that pencils at your number. The LOI is
non-binding, short (1–2 pages), and opens negotiation.

### LOI must include
- Buyer entity (and "and/or assigns").
- Purchase price **and structure** (all-cash vs. seller financing terms: down, rate, term,
  amortization, balloon).
- Earnest money amount and when it goes hard.
- **Due diligence period** (typically 30–60 days) and what you need access to.
- Closing timeline.
- Financing contingency (or "not contingent on financing" if you can swing it).
- Exclusivity / no-shop during DD.
- Brokerage acknowledgment.
- Expiration (give them a deadline to respond).

> Tie the price in your LOI to the **in-place NOI you computed** so you can defend it on the
> call: *"I'm at $X because that's an [8.5] cap on the trailing-twelve NOI after I reset taxes
> to my basis and put in a manager."* That sentence is what separates you from the LOI-blasters.

## SECTION 8 — COMPLIANCE (Read before you message anyone at scale)

You're contacting business owners at volume. This is legal and normal, but there are real rules.
**This is not legal advice — confirm with counsel before launching a mass campaign.**

- **CAN-SPAM (email):** Cold B2B email is legal **if** you (1) don't use deceptive subject
  lines/headers, (2) include a valid physical mailing address, and (3) include a working
  opt-out and honor it within 10 days. Build these into every template (they're in the Section 3
  email above).
- **TCPA (calls & texts):** This is the one to respect.
  - **Manual calls** to business numbers are generally lower risk. Avoid autodialers /
    prerecorded messages to **cell phones** without prior express consent.
  - **Cold SMS at scale to cell phones is high TCPA risk** (statutory damages $500–$1,500
    *per text*). Many park owners' "business" numbers are personal cells. **Recommendation:
    lead with email + manual calls; only text people who opted in or replied first.**
  - Scrub against the **National Do Not Call Registry** for any number that could be
    residential, and maintain your **own internal DNC/suppression list** — honor every opt-out
    immediately.
- **State laws:** Some states (e.g., FL, OK, WA) have stricter mini-TCPA texting/calling laws.
  Check your target states.
- **Direct mail:** Lowest regulatory risk; great for absentee owners.

**Safe-by-default stack:** Email-first (CAN-SPAM compliant) + direct mail + *manual* dialing,
with SMS reserved for opted-in conversations. Keep a master suppression list and never
re-contact an opt-out.

## SECTION 10 — DAILY/WEEKLY CADENCE & KPIs

Outreach is a numbers game *on top of* a quality filter. Track the funnel so you know what to
fix.

### Weekly rhythm (you or a VA)
- **Mon:** Enrich 25–50 new Tier-1 parks (skip-trace), load to GHL.
- **Tue–Thu:** Run sequences; make 15–25 *manual* calls/day; underwrite any new packages.
- **Fri:** Broker relationship touches; review pipeline; advance/kill stale deals.

### Funnel KPIs to watch
| Metric | Healthy signal |
|---|---|
| Contacts attempted / week | Volume is steady (e.g., 100–150) |
| Reply rate | Email 3–8%+; calls connect 20–30% |
| Conversation → financials | The real bottleneck — coach your ask |
| Packages → LOIs | You should LOI only ~10–20% (discipline) |
| LOI → accepted | Negotiation skill |
| **Deals underwritten / week** | This is the number that builds your skill |

> Early on, **optimize for reps, not closes.** Underwriting 3 real packages a week for two
> months will make you dangerous. The deals follow the skill.
