# Neurodivergent Community Living — Design Brief

**Project type:** Clustered residential community ("pocket neighborhood") for neurodivergent adults
**Location:** New Jersey, USA
**Care level:** High / behavioral support
**Scale:** 4 houses facing a shared commons · 8–12 residents per house · 32–48 residents total
**Status:** Concept / pre-design (verify everything with a licensed NJ architect + the licensing agency before construction)

> ⚠️ This is a design concept, **not** architectural, engineering, or legal advice. Room sizes, fire ratings, and staffing here are starting points. A NJ-licensed architect and the relevant licensing office must sign off before you build or lease.

---

## 1. The big decision first: how NJ will license this

This drives *everything* — room sizes, exits, sprinklers, staffing, even how many people you can legally put per building. In New Jersey there are **two different doors** you can walk through, and "ALF" means something specific here:

| Path | Agency / Rule | Who it's for | Typical size |
|------|---------------|--------------|--------------|
| **Assisted Living Residence (ALR)** | NJ Dept. of Health — **N.J.A.C. 8:36** | Aging / medical-support adults needing help with daily living | Larger facilities OK |
| **Community Residence / Group Home (DDD)** | NJ Div. of Developmental Disabilities — **N.J.A.C. 10:44A** | Adults with developmental disabilities / autism / behavioral support | **Small — often ≤ 4, occasionally up to ~15 in "supervised" settings** |

**The tension:** You said *high/behavioral support* + *8–12 per house*. For autism/developmental + behavioral needs, NJ's culture and licensing (and the clinical evidence) strongly favor **small households of 4–6**. Eight to twelve high-support residents in one open house tends to fail on three fronts: sensory overload, peer conflict, and supervision ratios.

**The design answer — the "pod" model:** Keep your 8–12 capacity *per building*, but split each house internally into **two self-contained households (pods) of 4–6**, each with its own living room, kitchenette, and quiet room, joined by a shared central core. You get the economics of a bigger building **and** the calm, family-scale feel that high-support residents need. This also maps cleanly onto licensing — each pod can read as its own small household.

**Building code note (NJ Uniform Construction Code / IBC):** Residential care for 6–16 residents *capable of self-preservation* is typically **R-4**; if residents need help to evacuate, it pushes to **I-1 (Condition 1 or 2)** and occasionally **I-2**. High-behavioral often means I-1 Condition 2. Expect **full sprinklers (NFPA 13/13R), a monitored fire alarm, and barrier-free (ADA / ANSI A117.1) design** regardless. Confirm the occupancy classification *early* — it changes corridor widths, door ratings, and exit count.

## 2. Design philosophy — what makes this *neurodivergent* housing

Pulled from autism/neurodivergent design research (e.g. the *Advancing Full Spectrum Housing* and *Autism Planning & Design* guidelines). Seven principles drive every decision below:

1. **Low arousal by default.** Calm is the baseline; stimulation is something residents *choose to add*, not something the building forces on them.
2. **Predictability & legibility.** Simple, repeating layouts. You can see where you're going. No mazes, no surprises.
3. **Right to retreat.** Every resident can withdraw to a private or quiet space *at any time* without asking permission or passing through a crowd.
4. **Sensory zoning.** Loud/active areas and quiet areas are physically separated, with transition "buffer" spaces between them.
5. **Passive supervision, not surveillance.** Staff can see what they need to see through sightlines and layout — the building does the work, so it doesn't *feel* watched.
6. **Durable & safe, but never institutional.** Robust, behavior-resistant construction that still reads as a *home* — warm materials, residential scale, no clinical corridors.
7. **Connection to nature & safe outdoors.** Secure courtyards, gardens, and a walking loop residents can use independently.

## 3. Each house — the program (≈ 8–12 residents, two pods)

Single-story is **strongly recommended**: no stairs to fall on or elope over, easier supervision, lower sensory load, simpler evacuation, full barrier-free access.

### Layout concept — an "H" plan
Two bedroom wings (the two pods) connected by a shared central core, wrapping a **secure private rear courtyard**.

```
        FRONT PORCH  →  faces the commons
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  POD A          CENTRAL CORE     POD B  │
   │  living     [ mudroom / entry ]  living │
   │  kitchenette[ great room:       ]kitch. │
   │  quiet rm   [ shared kitchen +  ]quiet  │
   │  4–6 BR     [ dining + hearth   ]4–6 BR │
   │  + baths    [ staff hub / med   ]+ baths│
   │             [ sensory room      ]       │
   └──────────────┐  SECURE REAR  ┌──────────┘
                  │   COURTYARD   │
                  └───────────────┘
```

### Room-by-room (per house)

| Space | Qty | Notes |
|-------|-----|-------|
| **Private bedroom** | 8–12 | ~130–150 sq ft each. Private = #1 conflict-reducer. Space for a bed, desk, personal storage. Operable, *limited-opening* windows with blackout + sheer layers. |
| **Bathrooms** | 1 per 1–2 residents | Ensuite or "Jack-and-Jill" between two rooms. Curbless roll-in showers, anti-scald valves, grab bars, slip-resistant floors. |
| **Pod living room** | 2 | One per pod (4–6 residents). The everyday social space — small enough to feel safe. |
| **Pod kitchenette** | 2 | Snacks, drinks, skill-building. Induction (no open flame), lockable as needed. |
| **Shared great room** | 1 | Central kitchen + dining + hearth. The "town square" of the house, for shared meals and events. |
| **Quiet / de-escalation room** | 2 | One per pod. Soft, dim, low-stimulation retreat. **Not** a seclusion room — a calm space residents *want* to use. |
| **Sensory room** | 1 | Multi-sensory: adjustable light, soft surfaces, swing/proprioceptive equipment, weighted options. |
| **Staff hub + med room** | 1 | Central, with sightlines down *both* wings. Locked medication storage, charting, secure records. |
| **Overnight staff room** | 1 | For awake-overnight or sleep staff per your license. |
| **Mudroom / transition entry** | 1 | A decompression buffer between outside and inside — bench, hooks, place to land. |
| **Laundry / storage / mechanical** | — | Acoustically isolated (HVAC + laundry are major noise offenders). |
| **Secure rear courtyard** | 1 | Private to the house, fenced, walkable, with shade and seating. |

**Rough area:** ~5,500–7,000 sq ft per house, single-story.

## 4. The sensory & safety detail spec (this is the heart of it)

### Acoustics — usually the #1 complaint
- Sound-absorbing ceilings & wall panels in all shared spaces; target a low reverberation time.
- **Acoustic separation between bedrooms** (insulated walls, solid-core doors, staggered/offset doorways so sound doesn't travel room-to-room).
- Locate laundry, mechanical, and dishwashers *away* from bedrooms and quiet rooms.
- Specify *quiet* HVAC; avoid buzzing ballasts entirely.

### Lighting
- Maximize **natural daylight**; control glare with layered shades.
- **No fluorescent tubes** (flicker + hum). LED with high flicker-free rating, **dimmable everywhere**.
- Warm color temperature in living/bedroom zones; tunable circadian lighting is a strong upgrade.
- Even, indirect light — avoid harsh pools and deep shadows.

### Color & materials
- **Muted, low-arousal palette** (soft neutrals, sage, muted blue). Save saturated color for small, intentional wayfinding accents.
- Matte finishes (gloss = glare). Avoid busy patterns and strong visual "noise."
- Natural materials (wood tones, soft textures) for a home-like, calming feel.

### Behavior-resistant / durable construction (high-support specific)
- Impact-resistant wall surfaces in high-traffic and de-escalation zones; reinforced corners.
- **Tamper- and ligature-resistant fixtures** in bedrooms/baths where indicated (anti-ligature hardware, recessed/sloped fittings).
- Tempered or impact glazing; **limited-opening, restricted window hardware**.
- Safe, non-toxic, washable, repairable materials throughout.
- No sharp edges; rounded corners on millwork.

### Safety & elopement (without feeling like a lockup)
- **Discreet door alarms / delayed egress** consistent with fire code (egress is never blocked — alarmed/monitored, not locked-in).
- Secure but *attractive* perimeter — landscaped fencing, not chain-link.
- Sightlines from the staff hub down both wings and into shared spaces.
- Wander-safe **looping circulation** (no dead-end corridors that trap or frustrate).
- Camera coverage *only* in shared/exterior areas — **never** bedrooms or baths (dignity + likely regulatory requirement).

### Wayfinding & predictability
- Each pod color-coded subtly for orientation.
- Consistent, repeated room layouts so the building "teaches itself."
- Visual schedules / labeling supported by clear, uncluttered surfaces.

## 5. The cluster — 4 houses facing each other

This is a **pocket neighborhood** (a.k.a. cottage cluster): homes turned **inward** around a shared green, with cars pushed to the edges. It's one of the best-proven layouts for community + safety + walkability.

```
                 [ PARKING ]                [ PARKING ]
        ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │                  HOUSE  (North)                │
        │              porch ↓ faces commons             │
        │   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐  │
        │   │                                         │  │
   H    │   │            THE  COMMONS                 │  │   H
   O →  │   │     sensory garden · raised beds        │  │ ← O
   U    │   │     walking loop · shade · seating      │  │   U
   S    │   │                                         │  │   S
   E    │   │        ┌───────────────────┐            │  │   E
 (West) │   │        │ COMMUNITY BUILDING│            │  │ (East)
        │   │        │ (shared hub)      │            │  │
        │   │        └───────────────────┘            │  │
        │   └─────────────────────────────────────────┘  │
        │              porch ↑ faces commons             │
        │                  HOUSE  (South)                │
        └───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                 [ PARKING ]                [ PARKING ]
            ←──── secure landscaped perimeter ────→
```

### Cluster principles
- **Front porches face the commons.** Every house has a porch onto the green — a *graduated* social space: residents can watch from the porch, step down to the green, or stay inside. Choice at every level.
- **Car-free interior.** Parking and the service/drop-off drive sit at the *perimeter*; the inner commons is walkable and safe for independent use.
- **Sightlines across the green.** Houses see each other and the commons → natural, passive "eyes on the green" without anyone feeling policed.
- **Short, safe walking distances** between houses and the community building.
- **One discreet, secure perimeter** around the whole site — so the *campus* is safe and residents can roam the interior freely, rather than locking down each individual house.

### The Commons (shared green)
- **Sensory garden** — varied textures, scents, raised accessible planting beds (great for skill-building & calm).
- **Walking / wandering loop** — a continuous accessible path residents can use to self-regulate (movement is a powerful tool).
- Shade structures, varied seating (social clusters *and* solo nooks).
- Smooth, glare-free, slip-resistant paving with clear edges.

### Community Building (the shared hub)
Centralizing the "high-activity" functions here keeps the houses calm.
- Multipurpose / event room (gatherings, programs).
- **Teaching kitchen** for life-skills & shared meals.
- Therapy / clinical rooms (OT, behavioral, telehealth).
- **Sensory gym** (movement, swings, proprioceptive equipment).
- Admin / staff base / training room.
- Optional wellness, salon, or vocational/maker space.

## 6. Staffing & operations (shapes the architecture)

High-behavioral support means richer ratios and 24/7 awake coverage. Architecturally that means:
- **Staff hub central in each house** with sightlines down both pods.
- A **campus staff base** in the community building for coordination, after-hours, and training.
- De-escalation/quiet rooms positioned so a resident can be *calmly redirected*, not paraded past peers.
- Door-alarm + nurse-call/notification systems wired throughout.

> Confirm minimum staffing ratios and awake-overnight rules with your licensing path (DDD vs. DOH) — they directly set the number of staff spaces you must build.

## 7. Build sequence / next steps

1. **Confirm the licensing path** (DDD community residence vs. DOH ALR). This locks occupancy class, room minimums, exits, and staffing. *Do this before any drawings.*
2. **Engage a NJ-licensed architect** experienced in I-1/R-4 residential care + a code consultant.
3. **Run the pod model past your clinical team** — validate pod size (4 vs. 5 vs. 6) against your residents' actual support needs.
4. **Site selection** — flat, walkable, near services/transit, zoning that permits group residential. Pocket-neighborhood layout needs a reasonably square/deep parcel.
5. **Pro forma** — capital cost, funding (Medicaid waiver / DDD funding / supportive housing programs), and per-resident operating model.
6. **Schematic design** → the 4-house cluster + community building from this brief.
